# Best Ford F-150 Lightning Model Years (Ranked)

The **Ford F-150 Lightning** launched for the **2022 model year** as the all-electric version of America's best-selling truck, pairing a familiar body-on-frame F-150 shape with a **dual-motor electric drivetrain**, a flat skateboard battery, and a usable front trunk. Buyers choose between a **Standard Range battery** (around 230-240 miles) and an **Extended Range battery** (around 300-320 miles), across trims from the work-focused **Pro** to the loaded **Platinum**. Because the Lightning is young, model-year differences come down to pricing, software maturity, and a few recalls rather than engine generations. This ranking covers the best Lightning model years and battery configurations, their range and capability, the issues to verify, and where the used-market value sits today.

## Direct Answer

The **best overall Ford F-150 Lightning is the 2024 Extended Range Lariat**, which combines the longest real-world range, mature **BlueCruise** hands-free driving, refined over-the-air software, and a strong remaining warranty after early production kinks were resolved. For shoppers focused on value, the **best value is the 2023 F-150 Lightning Pro Extended Range**, a fleet-oriented work truck with the big battery, plentiful onboard power outlets, and the steepest used depreciation of any Lightning, putting a long-range electric truck within reach. Be cautious with very early **2022** builds, which saw a battery-related stop-sale and several software-era recalls; verify all campaigns are closed.

## 1. 2024 Extended Range Lariat 🏆 BEST OVERALL

The **2024 Lightning** is the most polished version yet. Ford expanded **Extended Range** availability and added **Pro Power Onboard** flexibility while ironing out the early software and charging glitches. The **Extended Range battery** delivers an EPA-rated figure near **320 miles**, and the dual-motor setup produces up to **580 horsepower** and **775 lb-ft of torque**, with a 0-60 time around **4 seconds**. The Lariat trim adds a 15.5-inch screen, **BlueCruise hands-free** highway driving, and a comfortable interior. As the newest used option with the longest factory warranty remaining and the fewest open recalls, a clean 2024 Extended Range Lariat is the Lightning to buy if your budget reaches it.

## 2. 2023 F-150 Lightning Pro Extended Range 💎 BEST VALUE

The **Pro** is the no-frills work trim, and as a used buy it is the value champion. Pairing the **Extended Range battery** (around 320 miles) with the basic interior keeps the price low, yet you still get the full **dual-motor drivetrain**, **Pro Power Onboard** with up to **9.6 kW** of exportable power, and the same towing and payload hardware as pricier trims. **The best value is a 2023 Pro Extended Range**, which fleets bought in volume and which now sells used at a fraction of its original cost. You give up the big screen and BlueCruise, but gain a genuinely capable long-range electric truck at the lowest entry price in the lineup.

## 3. 2024 Extended Range Platinum

The **Platinum** is the flagship Lightning, and the 2024 model year is its best form. It bundles the **Extended Range battery**, a **22-speaker B&O sound system**, ventilated leather seats, a panoramic roof, and standard **BlueCruise**. Performance matches the rest of the Extended Range range with up to **580 hp** and quick acceleration. By 2024 the over-the-air updates had improved charging behavior, **trip planning**, and range estimation. The Platinum carries a high price even used, but for a buyer who wants the most luxurious electric truck with every feature, the 2024 Platinum is the pick. Depreciation is steep on this trim, which can make a lightly used one a relative bargain versus new.

Compared with the 2022 cars, the 2023 build benefits from refinements to the charging software and recall fixes applied during the year. Range, power, and towing match the 2024 model closely. With more depreciation behind it than a 2024 and fewer early-build worries than a 2022, a clean 2023 Lariat Extended Range is one of the smartest all-around Lightning purchases on the used market today.

As a first-year vehicle it carries the most software immaturity and the highest number of recalls, including the **2023 battery stop-sale** that paused deliveries. ## What to Watch For When Buying

The most important step when buying a used Lightning is to **run the VIN through Ford's recall lookup and the NHTSA database** and confirm every campaign is closed. The Lightning's history includes a **2023 battery stop-sale** tied to a manufacturing defect, plus several software-era recalls covering items like windshield wiper motors, tire-pressure labeling, and electrical control software. Early **2022** builds carry the most exposure, so documentation matters most there.

- **Battery and range:** choose **Extended Range (~320 miles)** for towing or long trips; **Standard Range (~240 miles)** suits local driving and home charging.
- **Charging hardware:** verify the **Ford Charge Station Pro** is included if the seller advertises home backup power; it is required for that feature.
- **Cold weather and towing:** expect significant range loss when towing or in winter; plan routes around real, not rated, range.
- **Software updates:** confirm the truck is current on **over-the-air updates**, which improved charging and range estimation over time.
- **Tires and brakes:** the truck is heavy, so inspect tire wear and suspension components closely.

## How to Choose

Match the Lightning to your priorities. For **the best blend of range, technology, and peace of mind**, the 2024 Extended Range Lariat is the answer, with mature software and BlueCruise. For **the best value**, a 2023 Pro Extended Range delivers the big battery and full capability at the lowest price, thanks to heavy fleet supply and depreciation. Feature-focused buyers should look at a **2023 or 2024 Lariat Extended Range**, while luxury seekers can find steep discounts on a **2024 Platinum**. Local-driving commuters can save with a **Standard Range XLT**. In every case, verify the battery campaign and all recalls are closed, and prioritize Extended Range if you tow.

## FAQ

**Which Ford F-150 Lightning years should I avoid?**
Be cautious with very early **2022** builds, which carry the most software immaturity and the broadest recall exposure, including the 2023 battery stop-sale. They can be fine buys, but only with every recall and the battery campaign confirmed closed through the VIN.

**What was the F-150 Lightning battery recall?**
In early 2023 Ford issued a **stop-sale and battery-related recall** after a manufacturing defect was found that could cause a battery fire. Affected trucks were repaired before delivery resumed. Always verify the campaign is complete on any used Lightning.

**What is the real range of the F-150 Lightning?**
The **Standard Range battery** is rated around **240 miles** and the **Extended Range battery** around **320 miles**. Real-world range drops meaningfully when towing, hauling, or driving in cold weather, so plan trips around actual conditions rather than the EPA figure.

**Is the F-150 Lightning Pro a good value?**
Yes. The **Pro** is the work-truck trim with the full dual-motor drivetrain and Pro Power Onboard but a basic interior, and it sells used at the lowest prices in the lineup. A 2023 Pro Extended Range is the best value way into a long-range electric truck.

## Bottom Line

The Ford F-150 Lightning is a capable electric truck whose model-year differences come down to **software maturity, recalls, and depreciation** rather than engine generations. The **2024 Extended Range Lariat is the best overall pick**, with the most polished software and the longest usable range, while the **2023 Pro Extended Range offers the best value**. When shopping any **2022** truck, confirm the **battery campaign and all recalls are closed** through the VIN. Choose **Extended Range** if you tow, and a sorted Lightning delivers F-150 capability, strong performance, and home-backup power.

## FAQ

**Why is the size of the largest-ever tech LBO relevant here?**
The largest PE take-private in tech history is Dell-EMC at roughly $67B in 2016, which required Michael Dell's rollover equity, Silver Lake co-invest, and a record $50B+ debt stack. ServiceNow's enterprise value sits about 3x that, so a deal would be roughly 3x the largest tech LBO ever attempted. Nothing since Dell-EMC has come close.

**What does the math look like at ServiceNow's current cap?**
At a $200B+ market cap, a take-private with a 25% control premium implies a roughly $250B equity check before debt assumption. LBO debt-financing capacity caps around $80-100B in today's high-yield and leveraged-loan markets, so you would need $150B+ of equity from a consortium — multiples of what any four-firm coalition has ever assembled.

**Why would Microsoft or Oracle block a PE auction?**
The moment ServiceNow puts itself in play, strategic acquirers like Microsoft or Oracle would bid the deal up 30-40% to either acquire or spoil the PE bid. PE cannot outbid a strategic at this scale. A Microsoft + ServiceNow combination would price above $300B but draw DOJ/EU antitrust blocks on workflow-monopoly grounds.

**What is the one scenario where a take-private could happen?**
A deep recession halving the market cap to roughly $100B, where a four-firm mega-consortium (Vista + KKR + Thoma Bravo + Silver Lake) could fund about $60B equity plus $50B debt. The structure would mirror Dell-EMC, with McDermott rolling equity as the "Michael Dell" of the deal and each firm contributing a $12-15B check, with the thesis being to fix Pro Plus pricing without quarterly earnings noise.

**What would the hypothetical PE cost-out playbook target?**
A 15-20% headcount reduction in non-revenue functions for roughly $1.5-2B annual EBITDA lift, collapsing 8 BU layers to 4, halving SVP count, and accelerating Pro Plus AI price increases of 30-40% without quarterly earnings drag. Non-core modules like HR Service Delivery and App Engine Studio could be sold to strategic buyers for $5-10B to fund a partial dividend recap.

## FAQ

**What three M&A scenarios does the article outline for Snowflake through 2028?**
The scenarios are a Cautious Tuck-In Path (selective AI, observability, and semantic-layer deals in the $50-300M range with no platform acquisitions), a Moderate Consolidation (one mid-size open-data/Iceberg-adjacent deal at $300-800M plus 3-4 smaller bolt-ons), and an Aggressive Iceberg Play ($1B+ to dominate open table formats, potentially acquiring Tabular). The article calls a Scenario 1-2 hybrid most likely. It expects 3-5 tuck-ins in 2025-26 and a 50% probability of 1-2 larger moves in 2027-28.

**How did Sridhar Ramaswamy's Feb 2024 mandate change Snowflake's M&A approach?**
The mandate shifted Snowflake away from Behn-era mega-acquisitions like Streamlit ($800M) and Neeva ($185M) toward selective, AI-forward deals, signaling a mature platform rather than portfolio-building. The article says this mandate "killed the acquisition-fueled growth narrative." It supports a disciplined, narrowly scoped M&A posture through 2028.

**Which specific companies does the article list as likely targets?**
Named targets include Hightouch (~$200-400M), Census (~$150-300M), Tabular (~$300-600M, Iceberg steward), Coalesce.io (~$150-250M), MotherDuck (~$200-400M, DuckDB serverless), AI observability peers like Fiddler (~$80-200M each), real-time pipeline vendors like Estuary and Upsolver, and semantic-layer plays like Cube.dev. Tabular carries a 35% 2028 probability and Hightouch/Census a 40% probability in the M&A table.

**Why does the article say the Iceberg bet keeps deals small?**
Because the Polaris foundation (2024) plus an open-source strategy means Snowflake doesn't need to acquire table-format IP, so partnerships suffice. The AI and Iceberg landscape is fragmented with no obvious $500M+ fortress target unlike Streamlit in 2022. The article also cites a market-multiples reset, noting Snowflake won't overpay at 8-10x revenue like the 2023-24 cohort.

**Which vendor intelligence sources does the article cite for its M&A thesis?**
It cites Pavilion (sales ops M&A patterns), Bridge Group (strategic CRO advisor benchmarking), Klue (competitive threat tracking on Databricks, dbt Labs, Airbyte), Force Management (sales strategy M&A rationale), and Mergermarket (independent M&A deal flow, guidance pricing, sponsor appetite data). These sources are said to confirm a tuck-in bias through 2026 and a mid-size consolidation window opening 2027-28.

# Substack Newsletter Launch — Setup + First Issue

**Why bother:** Owned audience > rented audience. Every PR site, every social platform, every search engine controls your distribution. A newsletter is YOUR list. Even 200 subscribers is a leverage point — when you ship the next thing, you don't have to re-pitch the world. You email the list.

**Honest expectation:** First issue gets 30-60 subscribers organically (from the launch announcement itself). Real growth comes from issues 2-12, where readers forward to colleagues. By month 6 you should have 500-2,000 if you ship every 2 weeks consistently.

---

## SETUP — 30 minutes total

1. **Sign up at substack.com** — free. Pick a publication name. Suggestion: **"PULSE Pulse"** (operator-focused weekly RevOps signal) OR **"The Machine Brief"** (one weekly entry-of-the-week from the autonomous library + your operator commentary).

2. **Custom domain (optional but recommended):** Substack lets you map a custom subdomain free. Suggested: `brief.pulserevops.com` or `pulse.pulserevops.com`. Adds ~$0 and keeps everything under your domain.

3. **Bio / About page** — paste:
> Operator-grade RevOps from a 22-year revenue executive. The Cliffs Notes for the discipline — distilled weekly from "The Machine," our autonomous AI knowledge engine. No vendor blogs. No listicle filler. No SEO sludge. Just what works in the field. Free, always.

4. **Logo** — use https://pulserevops.com/og-preview.jpg or generate a simple 200x200 from the wall-of-TVs screenshot.

5. **Welcome email** — Substack lets you set an auto-reply for new subscribers. Paste this:

> Welcome to the brief. Three things you should know.
>
> 1. This newsletter is short by design. One signal per issue. No filler.
> 2. Most issues will surface a single high-leverage entry from "The Machine" — the autonomous AI knowledge engine I built for RevOps — plus my operator commentary on what to actually do with it.
> 3. The whole platform is free at pulserevops.com. The Machine library is free. The CRM is free. The newsletter is free. The only ask: forward the issue to one operator who'd benefit, when something lands.
>
> First issue arrives in your inbox within the next ~24 hours.
>
> — Kory

6. **Schedule a publishing cadence:** Every 2 weeks for the first quarter. Don't try weekly until issue 8+. Discipline beats ambition.

## Why this newsletter exists

Most RevOps content is vendor blogs. Listicle filler. SEO sludge. "The 7 Trends Shaping the Future of Sales Operations in 2026." You read it; you don't act on it.

This newsletter is the inverse. Every two weeks, I'm going to surface **one high-leverage entry** from The Machine's library — and add my own operator commentary on **what to actually do with it.** That's the whole format. Short. Specific. Actionable.

## This issue's signal — Discount Governance

The Machine spent its second day camped on discount governance. The output across 15 entries: median SaaS win rates have collapsed from 23% in 2022 to 19% in 2024 (Gong), while annual-contract discounts have risen from 15% to 28% over the same period (Ebsta × Pavilion). 85% of B2B sales orgs operate without a formal discount-authority matrix. The estimated revenue leakage: **38% of potential ARR annually**.

I bundled the strongest insights into a pillar page yesterday: **The Full-Stack Discount Governance Playbook** — authority bands, comp-incentive alignment, CPQ enforcement, quarterly review cadence. Read it here: [pulserevops.com/pillars/discount-governance-full-stack](https://pulserevops.com/pillars/discount-governance-full-stack)

## My operator commentary on this signal

The piece every CRO misses: **comp-incentive alignment is more important than the authority matrix itself.** Most companies publish bands and stop. Then they wonder why discounting still creeps. The reason: AEs whose accelerators are tied to ACV alone will discount aggressively to close. AEs whose accelerators are tied to *margin-after-discount* won't. Same matrix, different behavior.

If you only do one thing this quarter, rewrite the comp plan to pay on margin, not gross. The lift is typically 3-5 points of average sold-price discipline within two quarters. That's not a tooling problem. That's an operator problem.

## SUBSTACK GROWTH PLAYBOOK (post-launch)

1. **Cross-promotion** — Substack has a "Recommendations" feature. Find 5 RevOps/sales/SaaS Substacks (Sangram Vajre, Pavilion, GTM Now, etc.) and recommend them. They'll often reciprocate, which exposes you to their lists.

2. **Notes (Substack's Twitter clone)** — Post 1-2 short "Notes" per week with insights from your library entries. They show up in the Substack-native feed and drive subscribers.

3. **Send issue archive to Hacker News (Show HN)** when you hit issue 6+ with a strong cumulative output. Format: "Show HN: I'm building an autonomous AI for RevOps — six issues in."

4. **Link from every library entry's `/knowledge/<id>` page** to the newsletter ("Get the Brief — one signal every 2 weeks") — captures the most engaged readers at the highest-intent moment.

# How Many Employees Should I Schedule Each Shift at My Auto Detail Shop?

## Direct Answer

You stop guessing and start dividing. The formula is **employees needed for a given shift = that shift's average gross profit / your agreed-upon daily gross-profit-per-rep target.** First, you and your leadership team agree on one number: the daily gross profit an average detailer should produce washing, polishing, running ceramic coatings, and selling interior and paint-correction packages - call it **$300 a day**. That is a floor, not a ceiling. Detailing is labor-rich with premium add-ons like ceramic coating and paint correction, so the per-person number runs above commodity service. Then you pull each shift's trailing three-to-six-month gross profit. If the Saturday opening shift averages **$1,500** in gross profit, then $1,500 / $300 = **5 detailers** on the floor that shift. If a slow Tuesday mid averages **$600**, you need **2**. You do that for every day part, then place those shifts against when the bookings actually land - the morning drop-off block, the appointment-heavy weekend, the late-day pickups - so the bodies are working when the money is. ## FAQ

**How do I set the daily gross-profit-per-person target for a detail shop?**
Look at your trailing shop-wide gross profit and your current crew headcount, then agree on the honest daily floor an average detailer should produce - most detail operators land somewhere between $250 and $450 a day once ceramic coatings and paint-correction packages are in the mix. Set it with leadership so it is a shared yardstick, not a number one manager invented, and revisit it once or twice a year.

**How do premium packages change the per-person target?**
Significantly. A detailer who sells and executes ceramic coatings and multi-stage paint corrections produces far more gross profit than one who only turns express washes. Build that premium-package margin into the target so the number reflects the full ticket, and consider a slightly higher target for your senior coating specialists.

**What if a shift's gross profit swings a lot week to week?**
Use a trailing three-to-six-month average by day of week to smooth the noise, and schedule to that baseline. For known spikes - spring and fall detailing season, pre-holiday gift bookings, a local car show weekend - add a manual bump on top of the calculated count rather than letting one wild week distort the whole average.

**Why staff to gross profit instead of car count or a fixed headcount?**
Car count and "we've always run three on Saturday" do not pay the labor bill - gross profit does. A day of express washes and a day of full ceramic-coating jobs look similar on a car-count board and very different on a P&L. Tying headcount to gross profit guarantees every scheduled detailer is covered by real margin.

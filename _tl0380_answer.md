## Why PLG Motions Stall at the Sales Handoff

A healthy PLG top of funnel can mask a completely broken middle. The stall almost always traces to one of these gaps.

1. **No definition of a product-qualified lead.** If you have not defined which product signals actually indicate sales-readiness - usage thresholds, team size, key feature adoption - then sales is handed a firehose of low-intent signups and reasonably concludes the leads are junk.
2. **Routing and timing are broken.** PLG signals are perishable. If a product-qualified lead is not routed to the right rep within hours, the moment passes. Most stalled motions have signals sitting in a system nobody is watching.
3. **The comp plan ignores PLG leads.** If reps are paid the same for a hard-won product-qualified expansion as for an outbound deal, they will chase whatever is easiest to control and let the PLG leads rot. Comp has to make working product-qualified leads worth their time.
4. **Nobody owns the seam.** Product owns the self-serve experience, sales owns the closed deal, and the handoff in between is an orphan. Without a single owner of the sales-assist motion, the most valuable signals in your funnel fall through the crack.

## What a Fractional CRO Actually Does to Fix the Handoff

A fractional CRO takes ownership of the seam between product and sales and builds the system that converts signals into revenue.

**Diagnose where signals die.** They trace the path from signup to product-qualified lead to sales touch to closed expansion, and find exactly where conversion collapses - usually the definition, the routing, the timing, or the comp. This map comes in the first weeks.

**Define the product-qualified lead.** They work with product and data to set the real signals of sales-readiness, so sales receives a focused stream of high-intent leads instead of a firehose, which alone changes how reps treat PLG leads.

**Build the sales-assist motion and routing.** They install the routing rules, response-time standards, and a sales-assist playbook so the right lead reaches the right rep fast, with a clear plan for the conversation. They make the handoff a defined process, not a hope.

**Realign comp and ownership.** They redesign comp so reps are paid to work product-qualified leads and expansion, and they assign a single owner to the PLG-to-sales seam so no signal is orphaned again.

## Fractional CRO vs Full-Time CRO vs Hiring More Reps

When PLG stalls at the handoff, the instinct is often to add reps, which usually makes it worse.

- **Hiring more reps** adds capacity to a broken process. If the handoff is the problem, more reps just means more people complaining the leads are junk while your acquisition cost climbs. You do not have a capacity problem, you have a system problem.
- **Full-time CRO** is hard to justify to fix what is fundamentally a process and alignment gap, especially when the rest of your motion may be working. Committing $300,000 to $500,000 all-in and equity is a heavy answer to a targeted problem.
- **Fractional CRO** is the precise fit: a senior operator who has aligned high-volume top-of-funnel with disciplined sales process diagnoses the seam, builds the definition, routing, sales-assist motion, and comp, and proves the conversion lift before you commit to a permanent hire. It is the surgical fix, not the sledgehammer.

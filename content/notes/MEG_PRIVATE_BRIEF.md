## 2. How they’re probably organized (divisions)

You were right that they almost certainly have **different motions**, even if marketing says “one MEG.”

**LIKELY org slices (typical for this shape of company):**

1. **Federal product / license sales** — Oracle (and Red Hat/Adobe) through federal vehicles; renewal-heavy; relationship + vehicle fluency.
2. **SLED sales** — all-50-states access story; NASPO / state vehicles; longer cycles, relationship + cooperative contracts.
3. **Consulting / SI delivery** — Oracle Cloud / hybrid modernization projects; billable utilization; different quota and margin math than resale.

6. **Emergent as a contract / brand vehicle** — common pattern: keep a subsidiary for **specific contract vehicles / past performance** so bidding rights and novations don’t get messy after PE.

**ASSUME:** Federal vs SLED vs consulting are **not** paid the same way and do **not** share the same attrition drivers. If you only learn one org map, learn **who owns renewals vs who owns net-new cloud/services**.

## 4. Industry they’re in (peg it)

**Industry:** U.S. **public-sector IT channel** — specifically the **OEM-aligned VAR/SI** layer between software vendors and government buyers.

**Economics that matter:**

- **Thin product margins** on resale; money is in **renewals, cloud subscriptions, services attach, and OEM incentives**.
- **Contract vehicles are oxygen** (GSA MAS, SEWP, CIO-CS-style vehicles, NASPO/state co-ops, agency BPAs). No vehicle fluency = no deal.
- **Relationships + clearances + past performance** transfer badly. When sellers/engineers leave, renewals and delivery wobble.
- **PE roll-up / platform pressure** is common in this space (aggregators consolidating; specialist platforms getting capital to grow services).

**Competitors sit in three buckets:**

| Bucket | Examples | How they win |
|--------|----------|--------------|
| **Master aggregators / VADs** | Carahsoft, Arrow/immixGroup, TD Synnex Public Sector (ex-DLT, etc.) | Vehicles + vendor breadth + channel enablement at scale |
| **OEM-specialist VARs/SIs** | Other Oracle/Red Hat specialists, boutique pubsec Oracle shops | Depth, certifications, delivery credibility |
| **Primes / big SIs** | Accenture, Deloitte, Booz, large cloud SIs | Services-led; sometimes partner/compete with VARs |

MEG’s lane is bucket 2 with aspirations to look more like a **platform** (services + multi-OEM + national coverage) without becoming a Carahsoft clone.

## 5. Issues they’re more than likely dealing with now

These are **LIKELY** for a PE-backed Oracle-heavy pubsec VAR ~3 years post-deal. Not insider knowledge.

### A. Mix-shift pain (product → cloud + services)
Renewal sellers who lived on license gross profit get asked to sell **cloud subscriptions + consulting**. Quota goes up; cycles get messier; delivery capacity becomes the bottleneck. Classic friction.

### B. Comp plan shock / “acceptable attrition”
PE-backed channel firms often rewrite plans: higher quotas, tighter SPIFs, more multi-product credit rules, delayed equity clarity. Industry pattern (see Optiv/KKR-type stories elsewhere): exec and seller exits spike after plan/culture change — sometimes treated as **acceptable loss**.

### C. Dual culture: Virginia Beach franchise vs “growth GTM”
Old Mythics muscle = Oracle relationships and delivery. New CRO narrative = AI / multi-cloud / national GTM. Middle managers get crushed between “protect the book” and “transform the motion.”

### D. Federal vs SLED imbalance
Federal can dominate brand and revenue; SLED is marketed as “all 50 states” but often under-resourced. Reps in weak states churn or sandbag.

### E. Delivery / utilization drag on services growth
You can’t grow consulting attach if staffing, clearances, and bench are thin. Sellers then stop selling services because delivery burned them.

### F. Aggregator squeeze
Carahsoft / immix / TD Synnex Public Sector own **breadth**. MEG wins on **Oracle depth**. Risk: OEMs route more volume through aggregators; MEG becomes “implementation partner” with less control of the transaction.

### G. Key-person risk on accounts
Pubsec renewals follow people. If Federal AMs or alliance leads leave for Carahsoft, an SI, or Oracle direct overlays, MEG feels it in 2–4 quarters — not immediately.

### H. Post-PE reporting burden
Forecast discipline, CRM hygiene, stage definitions, OEM deal registration accuracy — founders’ shops often under-invested here; PE forces it; sellers hate it; data still stays dirty for a year+.

## 6. Attrition — what it usually looks like in this industry

**LIKELY attrition hotspots for MEG-shaped companies:**

| Role | Why they leave |
|------|----------------|
| **Top federal AMs** | Quota up, credit rules change, recruiter calls from aggregators / primes / OEM |
| **Consulting sellers / solutions AEs** | Delivery can’t keep up → missed variable → exit |
| **Alliance / partner managers** | OEM politics + internal credit fights |
| **Cleared engineers / Oracle consultants** | Market pays up; PE cost control vs market rate |
| **Middle sales managers** | Caught between PE forecast theater and seller reality |

**What attrition costs here specifically:**
- Renewals slip or reprice badly  
- Vehicle / OEM deal-reg knowledge walks out  
- Clearance gaps delay delivery  
- Customer trust resets (“who is my Mythics person now?”)

**How companies actually fix it (not slogans):**
1. **Grandfather / transition comp** for 2–4 quarters when plans change — don’t cliff people overnight.  
2. **Renewal credit protection** for hunters who inherit a book (or split hunter/farmer cleanly).  
3. **Services attach SPIFs** paid on *accepted* SOWs / booked backlog, not vapor pipeline.  
4. **Retention equity or cash stay bonuses** for named strategic accounts — pool-level equity that nobody understands does nothing.  
5. **Manager span + forecast calendar** that doesn’t make Friday fire drills the culture.  
6. **Hire delivery ahead of services quota**, or services quota is fantasy.

## 7. How competition pays (structures, not “they make $X”)

Public salary sites are noisy. Use **structure**, not exact dollars.

### Aggregator pattern (Carahsoft-like)
- Often **high base / lower variable** relative to enterprise SaaS (RepVue-style snapshots have shown ~**60/40-ish** base/variable on some SLED AE roles — treat as directional only).  
- Variable tied to **bookings / revenue through vehicles**.  
- Culture: volume, vendor programs, coverage.  
- Attracts people who want **stability + brand + vehicle machine**.

### Specialist VAR / SI pattern (MEG’s peer set) — **LIKELY**
- **AEs / AMs (license + cloud):** roughly **50/50 to 60/40** base/variable at OTE; accelerators over 100%; SPIFs for strategic SKUs / cloud.  
- **Consulting / services sellers:** more **base-weighted** (e.g. **60/40–70/30**) with variable on **services bookings / margin / backlog**, sometimes paired with delivery utilization gates.  
- **Overlay / alliances:** lower personal quota, **MBO + influenced revenue**.  
- **SLED vs Federal:** Federal OTE usually higher; SLED more territory sprawl, more activity metrics.  
- **Leadership:** base + bonus on EBITDA / bookings; PE layer may add **equity options / profits interests** that feel opaque to individual contributors.

### Big SI / prime pattern
- More **salary + annual bonus**, less pure commission for many roles; sales specialists still OTE-based but with heavier process.  
- Compete for talent with **brand, clearance sponsorship, WLB pitch** — not always cash.

**Competitive pay pressure on MEG:** aggregators and primes can cherry-pick MEG’s best Oracle people; OEMs hire overlays; pure cloud SIs hire delivery. MEG has to win on **OTE clarity + account ownership + less bureaucracy**, or lose.

## 8. MEG’s compensation structure — what’s more than likely

**ASSUME / LIKELY (reconstructed from role mix + industry norms):**

### License / cloud AM (Federal)
- Base + commission on **product/cloud bookings**  
- Renewal book is a big chunk of attainability  
- Accelerators above plan  
- Possible clawbacks on cancellations / shorts  
- **Pain point:** if cloud and services don’t credit cleanly onto the same plan, reps optimize for what’s easy — usually renewals

### SLED AM
- Similar structure, **wider geography**, more cooperative-contract fluency  
- Often lower average deal size → activity + coverage matter more  
- **Pain point:** “all 50 states” marketing vs real capacity

### Consulting AE / services seller
- Variable on **services revenue or GM**  
- May share credit with product AM on attached deals (split credit is where wars start)  
- **Pain point:** delivery capacity gates earnings

### SEs / consultants
- Mostly salary + bonus; utilization / CSAT / billable targets  
- Not classic commission; attrition driven by rate + burnout + clearance leverage

### What PE often changes (watch for these)
- Raise quotas without raising OTE  
- Move from gross profit credit → revenue credit (or vice versa) without explaining  
- Cap accelerators  
- Add multi-product thresholds before any accelerator  
- Promise equity that vests on exit events nobody can time

## 9. Where competition is going

1. **Aggregators get broader** (more vendors, more vehicles, more AI/security catalogs). They don’t need to beat MEG on Oracle depth — they win by owning the **buy path**.  
2. **Cloud + AI modernization** is the narrative every pubsec seller is using (Paydos’s language matches the market, not a unique MEG invention).  
3. **Services attach is the valuation story** for PE exits — pure resale multiples are worse than recurring services / cloud.  
4. **OEM partner programs keep reshuffling** (Oracle’s partner model has already shifted once in the last decade). Specializations and deal registration rules can rewrite who gets paid overnight.  
5. **Talent war** for cleared Oracle / cloud people continues; PE cost control fights market wages.  
6. **Roll-ups continue** in gov IT services — platforms buy capability; culture and contract novations are the silent killers.

## 10. What I’d recommend *they* look at (your private “if I were advising MEG” list)

### Comp & territories
1. **Separate plans by motion** — Federal renewals, Federal net-new cloud, SLED, Consulting — stop forcing one plan to do four jobs.

### Attrition / ops
10. **Stay interviews** on top producers quarterly — cheap and ignored.  
11. **CRM that matches how pubsec deals actually move** (vehicle, clin, option years, renewal date) — not SaaS stage cosplay.  
12. **Manager forecast quality** as a managed metric (garbage forecast = PE distrust = worse plans next year).

### Competitive posture
13. **Don’t try to out-Carahsoft Carahsoft.** Win on Oracle (+ select OEMs) depth, delivery trust, and speed inside known accounts.  
14. **Partner with aggregators where needed** rather than pretending every deal must be MEG-transacted.

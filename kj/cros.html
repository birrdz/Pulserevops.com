KJ Opp — CROs

What It Is

CROs is a boutique fractional and interim CRO placement firm founded by Lisa Pettigrew. Unlike venture-scale consulting or PE-shop models, this is a network of experienced practitioners — not a marketplace or tech platform. You get paired with clients (usually $10M-$50M+ revenue, growth-focused) and work 15-20 hours/week on revenue strategy, sales team buildout, GTM transformation, and post-M&A integration. They position themselves as "practitioners who've built billion-dollar revenues," not theorists. Model is retainer-based, likely takes a placement cut on the engagement, but you own the client relationship.

Who's Behind It

  • Lisa Pettigrew (Founder & Managing Partner): 25+ years global P&L leadership across Accenture, Cognizant, DXC Technology, Avanade. Built $1B+ revenues, led 10+ post-acquisition integrations, strong alliance/channel strategy background. Australian expat, DC-based, runs a senior executive networking forum. Real operator with Fortune 500 chops — not a recruiter pretending to be a strategist.
  • Noah: Nearly 30 years commercial leadership, startups to Fortune 500, launched first-of-their-kind products and market categories.
  • Beth: 25+ years CRO experience across retail, healthcare, fintech, financial services. Built high-performing sales teams.
  • Katharine: Ex-Gartner (shaped sales process, delivered double-digit growth), then CRO at Sago (restructured sales org, drove growth).
  • Jenna: 25+ years driving revenue growth and sales transformations across technology, education, enterprise.

Signal: No venture pedigree, no PE backing mentioned. This is a boutique practitioner network, not a VC-scaled platform. That's either a feature (more alignment with your actual experience) or a bug (limited brand reach, slower growth).

How They Make Money

They place experienced CROs with client companies on retainer. Revenue model: likely 20-30% placement commission on the monthly retainer (you earn $15K-$30K/month, they take a cut). They may also charge clients a management fee on top. You bill directly; they handle sourcing, vetting, contracting, and ongoing account support.

Impact on you: You get a steady flow of introductions, they handle contracts and scope creep, they're incentivized to keep you busy (retainer stacking). But they also take a percentage off the top — you're not capturing 100% of client value.

What to Expect as a Fractional CRO There

  • Engagement length: 12-24 months typical; scope is strategic (GTM, sales team building, sales ops), not operational day-to-day. Some short-term (6-month) turnarounds for PE roll-ups or crisis situations.
  • Monthly retainer range: $15K-$30K/month (15-20 hrs/week). You'll negotiate based on stage, scope, and your track record. At Kory's level (22yr career, $3B scaled, 200+ org led), you're at the top of the market — likely $25K-$30K floor.
  • Comp structure: Pure retainer with monthly or quarterly billing. No equity typical (they avoid that complexity). Performance bonuses sometimes negotiated if you hit revenue milestones, but that's deal-by-deal.
  • Expected weekly hours: 15-20 hrs/week on-client. Add ~5 hrs/week internal (calls with Syndicate, new prospect intake, playbook updating).
  • Account ownership: You own the client relationship. Syndicate provides: lead flow, onboarding, contract admin, if needed, backup or consulting advice from other practitioners in the network. You are NOT a portfolio CRO in a pool — you're a named partner CRO who brings your personal brand.
  • Portfolio size: Likely 2-3 clients active at a time (40-60 billable hours/week cap). Room to scale to 4 clients if you want 60+ hrs/week.
  • Bench time risk: Low if demand is real. But fractional is a volume game — slow months happen. You'll need other income sources or be okay with utilization dips.

How to Be Successful There

  1. Get clarity on lead quality before signing: Ask Lisa directly: "Show me your last 5 placements — who were the clients, what was the scope, how long did they run?" Weak lead flow = slow ramp. Ignore rosy "pipeline" talk.
  2. Negotiate a floor retainer, not spot rates: Don't accept "we'll bill you out at $X/hr." Lock in a monthly floor ($25K+) even if client hours dip. Your time is too valuable to dance week-to-week. Push for 6-month minimum commitments with clients.
  3. Own the playbook, share it smart: Syndicate has a "Sales Leader Playbook" — ask to see it, adapt it for your style. But YOUR playbook (from $3B scaled experience) is your competitive edge. Don't give it away; use it to win more clients, faster.
  4. Screen clients ruthlessly: Not every prospect they send is worth your time. Say no to fractional gigs where the CEO isn't bought in, where the sales team is toxic, or where the ask is "implement a process I read about." You want operators who'll actually execute, not tire-kickers.
  5. Build peer relationships with other Syndicate CROs: This is a network, not a competitor's den. Noah, Beth, Katharine, Jenna — talk to them. Learn which client verticals work, which are nightmares, who gives quality referrals. Your peer network is the most valuable part of this deal.
  6. Lock in a 6-12 month financial baseline: Propose to Syndicate: "I'll commit to the network; in return, guarantee me X retainer from house accounts or give me 10 qualified leads/month with 80% close rates." Fractional without a floor is feast-famine.
  7. Track and publish case studies hard: Every successful engagement = a case study for the next prospect. Syndicate benefits from this too. Push them to let you build reference clients you can use for direct cold outreach (not all gigs are confidential). This compounds your personal brand over 12-24 months.

Red Flags / Questions to Ask Before Signing

  • "Who's actually using CROs right now?" Ask for 5 reference clients (not just Syndicate CROs as references). Call them. Did the CRO deliver? Would they hire again? If they won't give you founder references, walk.
  • "What's your utilization rate?" Ask Lisa: "Of your current CROs, what's the average billable hours/month? What % are at 100% utilization vs. 50%?" High bench time = a sign their lead flow is weak or their vetting is poor.
  • "What happens if I underperform or a client fires me?" If you blow an engagement, are they your advocate or do they quietly drop you? What's the path to recovery?
  • "How do you handle scope creep?" Fractional CROs often get sucked into 40+ hrs/week operationally. Does Syndicate enforce scope boundaries, or do they let clients milk the engagement?
  • "What's your take rate?" Don't assume — ask. If it's >30%, push back or negotiate down. At $25K/month, a 30% cut is $7.5K per client going to Syndicate — that's real money.
  • "Vertical focus vs. all-comers?" Does Syndicate specialize in verticals where you have credibility (SaaS, fintech, etc.) or do they send you into industries where you're starting from zero? Bad match = slower ramp.
  • "Is there a non-compete or exclusivity clause?" Can you take direct clients outside of Syndicate? What if a prospect you meet through them wants to hire you solo?

Comp Benchmark

Your profile: 22 years commercial leadership, $3B+ revenue scaled, 200+ org led. You're not junior; you're senior.

Market rates for your tier:

  • Full-time CRO role: $250K-$350K base + equity upside.
  • Fractional CRO at Chief Outsiders or similar network: $80K-$150K/year (roughly $7K-$12K/month) — but that's for mid-market practitioners, not your caliber.
  • Top-tier fractional CRO (your category): $250K-$350K/year ($20K-$30K/month) at 15-20 hrs/week.

Your negotiating floor:

  • $25K-$30K/month for 15-20 hrs/week (~$75K-$90K/year implied if you stack 2-3 clients).
  • Non-negotiable: 6-month minimum client commitments to avoid constant ramp-up.
  • Equity on side: If a client is Series A/B pre-revenue, consider 0.5-1% equity + $15K/month retainer (you're betting on them, not betting on Syndicate).

If Syndicate pushes back with $15K-$18K/month: They're treating you like a mid-market practitioner. Push. You have the experience to command top-tier rates. If they won't move, their lead flow is weak or they don't believe in their own pipeline.

Bottom Line

Qualified yes, but only if you validate the lead flow and lock in a floor retainer. CROs is legit (Lisa Pettigrew is a real operator, team looks solid, no red flags), but it's a small, boutique network — not a venture-scaled platform like Chief Outsiders. That means slower growth but also more personalized matching. The risk: you get placed with one or two weak clients and spend 6 months with low utilization. The upside: if lead flow is real, you can earn $250K-$350K/year at 15-20 hrs/week and build a personal brand as a recognized fractional CRO. Ask hard questions before signing, get 5 founder references, and don't accept a comp package below your tier.

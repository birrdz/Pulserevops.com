# Arturo — Company Overview (Internal Research Brief)

> Internal only, for job prep. Facts only. **Arturo is defunct** (ceased operations 2025 following an asset sale). All descriptions below are historical / past-tense.

## What Arturo was

Arturo was a property-intelligence (property-analytics) company that used artificial intelligence and computer vision on geospatial imagery to derive physical property characteristics and predictive risk analytics, primarily for the insurance industry. Its customers spanned property & casualty (P&C) insurance, reinsurance, lending, and securities, and it applied its data to underwriting, pricing, portfolio risk management, catastrophe response, and claims.

The company originated as a deep-learning initiative inside American Family Insurance and spun out as an independent company in 2018, following more than three years of internal research into applying AI and deep learning to satellite, aerial, drone, and ground-level imagery. ([Coverager — Arturo Announces Spin-out from American Family](https://coverager.com/arturo-announces-spin-out-from-american-family/); [Carrier Management, 2019](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2019/07/29/195946.htm)) Its original headquarters was in Denver, Colorado, and in November 2024 it relocated to South Jordan / Salt Lake City, Utah. ([TechBuzz News](https://www.techbuzznews.com/arturo-an-innovative-property-insurance-imaging-ai-company-moves-to-salt-lake-city-2/); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/arturo-1))

## What it did — product and technology

Arturo combined satellite, aerial, ground-level, and multispectral imagery with geospatial data, customer portfolio data, and machine-learning models on a single platform. From imagery, it derived a reported 100+ property attributes — including roof condition and damage, roof composition, exterior structures, and presence of solar panels. ([InsTech — Property intelligence from the sky](https://www.instech.co/knowledge-centre/arturo-property-intelligence-from-the-sky/); [Technori](https://technori.com/2020/03/18651-how-arturo-is-using-ai-to-improve-underwriting/fiske/))

Stated use cases included:

- **Underwriting and pricing** — supplying verified property characteristics to inform risk selection and rating.
- **Address-based quote pre-fill** — API delivery of property attributes to reduce customer-facing questions at point of sale.
- **Property "health checks"** and portfolio-level risk assessment.
- **Claims and catastrophe response.**

In October 2024 the company also announced change-detection technology for insurers, comparing imagery over time to flag property changes. ([Business Wire — Change Detection](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241014874587/en/Arturo-Unveils-Groundbreaking-Change-Detection-Technology-for-Insurers))

## Value proposition

Arturo's positioning was that it converted imagery into decision-ready property data at speed and scale, letting insurers reduce manual property assessment and remove customer-facing questions. A widely cited demonstration of this was its Suncorp Group project in Australia: Arturo delivered 30+ unique property characteristics across nearly 9 million records — described as covering essentially every residential property in Australia — in under 48 hours. Suncorp reported the resulting data removed roughly 50% of property-attribute questions from its online platforms and call centres. ([Business Wire — 9M Australian Properties](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201028006203/en/Arturo-Delivers-Insights-Across-Nearly-9M-Australian-Properties); [insuranceNEWS.com.au](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/insurtech/suncorp-halves-disclosure-list-with-images-of-9-million-homes))

## Industry and market

Arturo operated in the property-intelligence / geospatial-analytics segment serving P&C insurance (an "insurtech" data-and-analytics vendor). Reported and named competitors in this space included Cape Analytics, Zesty.ai, Betterview (which integrated with Nearmap), Verisk, HOVER, CoreLogic (later Cotality), and Insurdata. ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/arturo-1))

## Leadership

- **John-Isaac "JC" Clark** — early CEO and co-founder; previously head of Commercial Product at DigitalGlobe, with a geospatial background.
- **Marty Smuin** — became CEO in September 2023 (the company's third CEO) and remained CEO through the company's closure. ([Insurance Business — Arturo names new CEO](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/arturo-names-new-ceo-463997.aspx))

## Funding and investors

Arturo raised approximately $47 million in total across its life. Disclosed rounds included a **$8M Series A (2020)** and a **$25M Series B (2021)**, the latter led by Atlantic Bridge Capital. Named investors included American Family, Atlantic Bridge, Crosslink Capital, IAG Firemark Ventures, and RPS Ventures. ([Business Wire — Series A](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200407005756/en/Arturo-Secures-%248-Million-in-Series-A-Funding-to-Advance-ML-and-AI-in-the-Insurance-Market); [Carrier Management — Series B $25M](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2021/04/27/219843.htm))

## Clients and partnerships

Reported clients and partners included Suncorp and IAG in Australia, Hippo (US, 2019), and USAA. In October 2020, Arturo announced a partnership with LexisNexis Risk Solutions to deliver roof-condition solutions for US home insurers. ([Carrier Management — Hippo](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2019/07/11/195322.htm); [LexisNexis Risk Solutions press release](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/about-us/press-room/press-release/20201022-arturo-alliance))

## Current status — defunct (2025)

Arturo **ceased operations in 2025 following an asset sale.** CEO Marty Smuin confirmed the closure, and reporting on July 30, 2025 stated the company had completed an asset sale and wound down; its website went dark. ([The Insurer — Arturo ceases operations after asset sale](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/arturo-ceases-operations-after-asset-sale-ceo-says-2025-07-30/); [Coverager — Arturo's website goes dark](https://coverager.com/arturos-website-goes-dark-following-asset-sale/))

The **exact legal entity that acquired Arturo's assets is not publicly confirmed** in the sources reviewed. Internal notes point to a "Risk Solutions" buyer, which may relate to Arturo's prior LexisNexis Risk Solutions partnership, but this identification could not be verified against a primary source and should be treated as unconfirmed pending further verification.

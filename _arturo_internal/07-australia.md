# Arturo — Australia Operations (internal research brief)

> Internal only, for Kory White's job prep. Facts only, past tense. Arturo ceased operations in 2025.

## Overview

Australia and New Zealand were among Arturo's earliest and most significant markets outside the United States. Two Australian insurers anchored that footprint: **IAG** (Insurance Australia Group), which became a shareholder through its corporate venture arm, and **Suncorp Group**, which became a large-scale client. Arturo remained headquartered in the US (Denver, CO, later South Jordan, Utah); the Australian relationships were run as investor and customer engagements rather than from a separately reported Australian corporate office.

## IAG / Firemark Ventures investment

IAG — Australia's largest general insurer — invested in Arturo through **Firemark Ventures**, its corporate venture fund. The investment was announced in **April 2020** and made Arturo the fund's **first portfolio company from outside the United States**.

Scott Gunther, then a Firemark Ventures director, said: *"We are pleased to be Arturo's first investor outside the US and look forward to working with the team to bring their insights and capabilities to our Australian and New Zealand insurance operations."* Arturo co-founder/CEO John-Isaac "JC" Clark framed the technology — deriving property information from satellite, aerial and drone imagery without people on the ground — as *"revolutionary and necessary in light of current global circumstances"* (the announcement coincided with early COVID-19 restrictions). The stated goal was to explore how Arturo's analytics could support IAG's quote, pricing, underwriting and renewal stages, and help customers during major weather events.

IAG **doubled down** by participating in Arturo's **$25 million Series B** round (announced **April 2021**, led by Atlantic Bridge Capital), making IAG Firemark Ventures a repeat investor. IAG appears on Arturo's investor roster alongside American Family, Atlantic Bridge, Crosslink Capital and RPS Ventures.

## Suncorp engagement — ~9M properties in under 48 hours

Arturo's flagship Australian deliverable was for **Suncorp Group**, one of Australia and New Zealand's leading insurance and banking companies. Announced in **October 2020** (Business Wire, Oct 28, 2020), the project delivered **more than 30 unique property characteristics** across **almost 9 million records — roughly 85% of Australia — in under 48 hours.**

To do this, Arturo **trained its deep-learning models specifically for Australian conditions** for the first time, and built a "fully auto-scalable" platform to run the models at that volume. Underlying aerial imagery was sourced from **Aerometrex and GIC** and refreshed roughly **every 4–6 months**. Extracted attributes included roof type, swimming pools and other structural features.

Suncorp used the data to **automatically populate customer information** during quoting and renewal, eliminating roughly **half** the disclosure questions previously asked. Katherine Carmody, Suncorp's EGM Digital Distribution, said: *"The questions don't exist anymore. This isn't pre-population, this is complete removal."* The initiative was shortlisted for an Ashton Media award for technology's role in improving customer experience. Suncorp addressed privacy questions by comparing the imagery precedent to Google Earth.

## Australian office / staff

Public reporting confirmed an Australian **market presence** (an anchor investor and a nationwide client deployment) but did **not** verifiably document a dedicated Australian corporate office or named local headcount. Arturo remained US-headquartered throughout its life. Claims of an Australian "presence" in secondary coverage (e.g., insurtech newsletters) refer to the Suncorp delivery and IAG relationship rather than to a separately reported Australian legal entity or staffed office; a specific office location and employee count could not be verified and is therefore omitted.

## Australian insurtech context

Arturo's Australian traction fit a broader local shift toward imagery-and-AI property intelligence for underwriting and cat response. Suncorp separately worked with satellite/flood-analytics providers (e.g., an ICEYE flood-insights case study references Suncorp), and IAG's Firemark Ventures continued backing data/analytics startups (including Digital Agriculture Services). Note the later ownership context: **Suncorp's Australian home/motor insurance business was acquired by ANZ** (Suncorp Bank sale completed 2024), part of consolidation reshaping the market Arturo had served. Competitors touching the same Australian/NZ market included Nearmap (aerial imagery, integrated with Betterview), Cape Analytics and Zesty.ai.

## Bottom line

Australia was disproportionately important to Arturo's early story: its **first non-US investor** (IAG Firemark Ventures, 2020, repeated in the 2021 Series B) and one of its **largest single deliveries** on record (the Suncorp ~9M-property, 85%-of-Australia run in under 48 hours, Oct 2020). Both relationships predate the company's 2025 wind-down and asset sale.
